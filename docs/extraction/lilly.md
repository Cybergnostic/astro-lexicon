# William Lilly — extraction ledger

Source: *Christian Astrology*, all three books, digital retype of the 1647 edition in the initial corpus.

## Sequential coverage

- PDF pp. 46–68: Book I, Chapters I–V, introductory astronomy/astrology, ephemeris use, figure erection and planetary motion, reviewed sequentially.
- PDF pp. 68–77: Book I, Chapters VI–VII, houses, quadrants, angular/succedent/cadent strength and house significations, reviewed sequentially.
- PDF pp. 78–105: Book I, Chapters VIII–XIV, planetary descriptions and dignity tables, reviewed sequentially. Long natural-signification catalogues were not promoted wholesale.
- PDF pp. 105–122: Book I, Chapters XV–XVII, planetary forms, sign classifications, antiscions and sign descriptions, reviewed sequentially. Long country, disease and body catalogues were treated as applications rather than lexicon entries.
- PDF pp. 122–141: Book I, Chapters XVIII–XIX, essential and accidental dignities; aspect terminology; application, separation, prohibition, refranation, translation, reception, peregrination, void course, frustration, hayz, combustion, under-the-beams, cazimi, besiegement and motion, reviewed sequentially.
- PDF pp. 142–149: Book I, Considerations before Judgment and Chapters XX–XXI, radicality, querent/quesited/significator doctrine, modes of perfection and derived houses, reviewed sequentially and completely.
- PDF pp. 150–151: dignity table / title transition. Book II substantive text begins on PDF p. 152.

## Editorial notes

Only definition-level or substantively explanatory evidence is promoted. Lilly's very large catalogues of planetary and sign correspondences, diseases, herbs, animals, countries, body descriptions and similar material remain part of the searchable corpus but are not automatically converted into encyclopedia entries.

Book I is unusually important for the lexicon because Chapter XIX gives direct compact definitions of many technical terms later used throughout the horary judgments. These are kept source-specific even where Bonatti, al-Biruni or Antiochus use related but non-identical mechanisms.

Lilly's `Partile` / `Platick` distinction, antiscion doctrine, definition of a radical question, and explicit derived-house procedure are retained as substantive lexical material.
