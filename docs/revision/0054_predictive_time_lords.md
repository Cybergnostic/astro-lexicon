# Revision batch 8 — predictive and time-lord systems

Implemented in `db/seed/0054_cross_source_revision_predictive_time_lords.sql`.

## Firdaria normalized across the medieval sources

**Firdaria / Fardār** is now one canonical entry supported by Abū Maʿshar, al-Bīrūnī and Bonatti.

The stable cross-source structure is:

- unequal major planetary periods;
- the sequence begins from the sect luminary, Sun by day and Moon by night;
- the principal ruler participates with successive planetary subperiod rulers.

The exact period values and node treatment remain source-level information rather than being overloaded into the canonical definition.

## Lilly’s Alfridary deliberately NOT merged with Firdaria

Lilly explicitly calls his system the **Lords of the Septennial Years**, vulgarly the **Lords of the Alfridary**. Each ruler receives exactly seven years.

That mechanism is not the medieval Firdaria described by Abū Maʿshar, al-Bīrūnī and Bonatti, whose planetary periods are unequal. The terms are therefore linked as historically related vocabulary but remain separate canonical entries.

This is another false-friend case where similar historical naming is not enough to prove technical identity.

## Planetary Years

**Planetary Years** is now publication-ready as the doctrine of several numerical year classes assigned to each planet: least/small, mean/middle, great and greatest. Exact values and uses remain in source interpretations.

It is explicitly distinguished from Firdaria: planetary years are numerical quantities; Firdaria is an ordered sequence of period rulers.

## Annual revolution family

The generic **Annual Revolution** remains an umbrella technique: the Sun returns to a specified starting longitude, which can belong to a nativity, mundane year or another inception.

Bonatti’s duplicate **Revolution of the Year of a Nativity** is merged into canonical **Solar Revolution / Natal Annual Revolution**. Lilly’s **Annual Conversion** is retained as an alias.

Relations now distinguish:

- Solar Revolution — natal subtype of Annual Revolution;
- Revolution of the Year of the World — mundane subtype of Annual Revolution.

## Lord of the Year versus other annual rulers

**Lord of the Year** remains quality 4 because the role is stable but its selection algorithm varies strongly across sources. It is defined as the principal planetary ruler assigned to a year, not simply the ruler of the annual Ascendant.

**Lord of the Revolution** remains separate. Bonatti explicitly distinguishes the ruler of the entire year from rulers of half-year or quarterly revolutions.

Lilly’s statement that **Lord of the Year = Chronocrator** is retained only as a source-scoped alias, not as a universal equation of every historical chronocrator system.

## Sālkhudāy / Lord of the Profection

Bonatti’s **Sālkhudāy** remains a separate quality-5 entry: it is specifically the ruler of the sign reached by the annual profection and participates with the annual-revolution ruler.

It is linked as a specialized form of Lord-of-Year language, but is not flattened into the generic Lord of the Year because the recognition rule is explicitly profectional.

## Other period systems

Publication-ready or strong entries now include:

- Lord of the Orb / Circle — Bonatti’s house-ruler + planetary-hour yearly sequence;
- Triplicity Division of Life — three successive life portions ruled by the three triplicity rulers;
- Direction / Period by Term Lords — successive bound rulers governing successive spans from the Ascendant.

The term-lord period doctrine is linked to **Jarbakhtar / Divisor** but not merged with it. The later Jarbakhtar treatment is explicitly a more developed direction through bounds using ascensional measurement.

## Stable predictive techniques

Profection, Solar Revolution, Primary Direction and Transit receive final A-grade/reviewed treatment where their source definitions are direct. Profection and Solar Revolution are explicitly related as techniques used together rather than substitutes for one another.

No new quotations were stored. Lilly’s Alfridary wording is very clear, but the paraphrase and page locator are sufficient and avoid adding quotation merely for decoration.
