## Part V extraction notes

### Chapter 1 — planetary shares
Abū Maʿshar explicitly places house, exaltation, triplicity, terms, decans, joy, fall, detriment, and special degree classes inside a broad doctrine of planetary shares in the signs. This umbrella category is broader than a later simplified list of essential dignities.

### Chapters 2–4 — houses
The canonical house assignments are stated and then justified through several competing explanatory traditions. Abū Maʿshar criticizes some explanations, presents an expanded Ptolemaic rationale, and then a Hermetic/Agathodaimon arrangement. Detriment is identified as the seventh sign from the house. Chapter 4 also preserves the Lot of the Nature of the Planets.

### Chapters 5–7 — exaltations and falls
Several rationales for exaltation are preserved rather than merged. Ptolemaic arguments use seasonal increase/decrease and planetary quality; the Hermetic account emphasizes beginning, culmination, decline, planetary ordering and the strongest portion of a sign. Fall is the opposite sign of exaltation. Chapter 7 separately develops specific exaltation degrees, so sign-level exaltation and exaltation degree remain distinct objects in the ontology.

### Chapters 8–13 — five systems of terms
Abū Maʿshar explicitly enumerates five systems: Egyptian, Ptolemaic, Chaldean, Asṭraṭū, and Indian. Asṭraṭū is unusual because the luminaries receive terms as well. The Chaldean distribution is not reproduced because Abū Maʿshar regards the transmitted material as confused and lacking agreement. He explicitly prefers the Egyptian terms and says earlier learned astrologers commonly used them in judgments.

### Chapter 14 — triplicity rulers
The full day/night/participating scheme is given: Fire — Sun/Jupiter/Saturn; Earth — Venus/Moon/Mars; Air — Saturn/Mercury/Jupiter; Water — Venus/Mars/Moon. Mercury has a special participation in Virgo. The source connects the ordering with planetary gender and ḥayyiz.

### Chapters 15–16 — decans
The Persian/Babylonian/Egyptian system divides every sign into three ten-degree decans and assigns rulers in planetary-sphere order. The Indian darīǧān uses the same threefold structure but assigns the first to the sign lord, the second to the lord of the fifth sign, and the third to the lord of the ninth. These are related systems, not silently identical rulership doctrines.

### Chapter 17 — ninths / nawbahr
Each sign is divided into nine equal parts of 3°20′. Abū Maʿshar presents more than one rulership procedure and explicitly prefers the first. The term nawbahr is retained as historical vocabulary alongside the structural English label “ninth”.

### Chapter 18 — dodecatemoria and degree sign-natures
A dodecatemorion is one of twelve 2.5° divisions of a sign, each taking the nature of a successive zodiacal sign. The chapter also preserves a separate one-degree-at-a-time Hermetic scheme in which sign natures repeat through the thirty degrees. These two procedures must not be collapsed.

### Chapter 19 — masculine and feminine degrees
Three methods are preserved. Abū Maʿshar says agreement of two or all three methods strengthens the classification. Degree gender is therefore distinct from the masculine/feminine classification of the sign as a whole.

### Chapter 20 — bright, dusky, dark and empty degrees
The source preserves a four-class degree-quality doctrine, but the edition shows instability in the exact vocabulary for dusky, dark, shadowy, and smoky degrees. The database therefore records the classes while flagging the terminology as textually uncertain rather than pretending the English labels are perfectly fixed.

### Chapter 21 — planetary wells
Specific degrees are called wells. A planet exactly in one is said to lose splendour and become weak in indication. Abū Maʿshar acknowledges disagreement over the exact lists and records what he describes as broad Persian-Egyptian agreement.

### Chapter 22 — degrees increasing good fortune
Certain degrees are described as amplifying already fortunate indications when occupied by relevant points such as the Moon, ascendant, or Lot of Fortune. The doctrine is conditional and multiple lists are preserved; these degrees are not treated as automatic guarantees of fortune.

## Editorial observations

1. `Planetary Share` is an important umbrella concept and must not be equated simply with essential dignity.
2. Source disagreement is data. Competing explanations of domicile and exaltation should remain attributable to their traditions rather than be reconciled into one modern rationale.
3. Sign-level exaltation and exaltation degree are separate concepts.
4. The five terms systems are historically distinct systems, not aliases for a single table.
5. Asṭraṭū is unidentified in the edition; the database should preserve the name and uncertainty rather than guess an identity.
6. Darīǧān is structurally related to the decan but has a different rulership system; relation is safer than synonymy.
7. Dodecatemoria and the separate degree-by-degree sign-nature cycle are distinct procedures.
8. The terminology of bright/dusky/dark/shadowy/smoky degrees is textually unstable in the translation tradition and should carry editorial warnings.
9. Degree lists are stored as source-specific doctrine. Where Abū Maʿshar reports disagreement, the public lexicon should say so.
10. `Joy` is named in Part V Chapter 1 as a share but not defined sufficiently in this Part to justify a full source-specific definition yet; defer expansion until a dedicated explanation is encountered.
11. Historical natural, medical, geographical, and cosmological claims are represented as historical source doctrine, not as modern scientific claims.
12. The source edition is copyrighted; extracted public material is paraphrased and cited rather than reproduced at length.
