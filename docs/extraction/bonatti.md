## Editorial rule applied

Only evidence-grade A or B material is stored as a source interpretation. Polemical repetition, historical name lists, incidental examples, long correspondence catalogues and mere mentions are skipped. Translator/editor commentary is not attributed to Bonatti.

Bonatti’s terminology around “astrology” and “astronomy” is source-specific and not normalized silently to modern usage. Likewise, overlapping medieval vocabulary such as fall/descension/detriment is preserved in interpretation notes rather than silently harmonized.

The Second Tractate, Part III pass preserves Bonatti’s own definition of Aym / “own similitude” independently from Robert Hand’s editorial identification with hayz.

The Third Tractatus adds especially important operational definitions for Almuguea, Alitifal/application, Dustoria/Hayz, planetary orbs, latitude application, void course versus feral/rustic condition, transfer of nature, return and abscission of light, prohibition, reception as donation of virtue, return of virtue, refranation, contrariety, frustration, besiegement, and the opening of the gates. These are kept distinct where Bonatti distinguishes their mechanisms.

Treatise 4 adds the conjunction hierarchy used in mundane astrology, the special Saturn–Mars conjunction in Cancer, the distinct combust/incombust hour cycle after a lunation, the Moon’s temporal duodena, and Bonatti’s syzygy-based Ascendant rectification procedure. The combust-hour doctrine is kept separate from planetary combustion, and the Moon’s duodena is kept separate from zodiacal twelfth-parts.

Treatise 8.1 adds explicit definitions of annual revolution, the world-year revolution and nativity-year revolution; a detailed hierarchy for selecting the Lord of the Year and the significator of the king; the seven-clime framework; and Bonatti’s explicit distinction between a Lord of the whole Year and a Lord of a shorter half-year or quarterly Revolution.

The final chapters of Treatise 8.1 add usable doctrines for exact fixed-star conjunction, benefic and malefic fixed-star classes, comet judgment, eclipse-duration timing, and the modifying role of reception in annual judgment. The extensive planet-in-sign/house, fixed-star, regional, weather, disease and political prediction catalogues were reviewed but not promoted into a large number of weak lexicon entries. Historical fixed-star coordinates are treated as epoch-dependent data rather than timeless positions. `al-mubtazz` is mentioned but not defined sufficiently here, so no interpretation was created from this occurrence.

Treatise 10 adds a compact meteorological vocabulary: Mercury and the Moon as convertible planets in this context, wet/dry/common lunar mansions, and the four lunar dichotomies used as weather checkpoints. The many mansion names, weather recipes, cloud/halo/rainbow observations and repeated planet combinations were reviewed but not promoted into separate entries unless they supplied reusable technical doctrine.

Treatise 5 adds source-level definitions and refinements for rooted/radical questions, Bonatti’s “own light,” planetary impediment and escape from malefic contact, void course versus ferality, reception, transfer of light, cazimi, under-the-rays conditions, peregrination, and the specialized participators derived in the 146th Consideration. It also gives a substantial doctrine of fixed-star gifts: unusually large elevations that are less durable in human subjects than planetary gifts. The many repeated aphorisms, natal catalogues and biographical anecdotes were read but not promoted into weak standalone terms.

Treatise 6 Part I adds Bonatti’s explicit distinction between absolute and determinate questions, a source-defined universal question, and the abscissor or planet which cuts off. Its introductory chapters give the three principal modes of perfection—direct joining of significators, transfer of light, and collection of light—and repeatedly qualify perfection by reception, planetary condition and the state of the receiving planet. The house-specific chapters were reviewed as applications of these principles rather than converted into a large catalogue of weak entries. The illness section also gives a substantive critical-day procedure using the Moon at seventh-day intervals; this is retained as a Bonatti interpretation of the existing `critical-days` term rather than as a new generic medical rule.

Treatise 7 adds a direct definition of an election as the deliberate choice of a fitting beginning for an intended action rather than a cause which produces the action itself. It distinguishes the root of an election (preferably a nativity or horary question), the adaptation of signs and planets, and the exact inception of a work as its first intrinsic act rather than a preparatory act. It also gives a substantial timing doctrine in which planetary motion, modality, ascensional type, element, angularity and the distance between significators are judged relative to the natural timescale of the matter. The extensive house-by-house recipes were reviewed but not converted into weak standalone lexicon entries.
