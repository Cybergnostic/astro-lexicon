## Editorial notes

Only definition-level or substantively explanatory evidence is promoted. Lilly's very large catalogues of planetary and sign correspondences, diseases, herbs, animals, countries, body descriptions and similar material remain part of the searchable corpus but are not automatically converted into encyclopedia entries.

Book I is unusually important for the lexicon because Chapter XIX gives direct compact definitions of many technical terms later used throughout the horary judgments. These are kept source-specific even where Bonatti, al-Biruni or Antiochus use related but non-identical mechanisms.

Lilly's `Partile` / `Platick` distinction, antiscion doctrine, definition of a radical question, and explicit derived-house procedure are retained as substantive lexical material.

Book II adds an explicit source-specific rule for the moment a horary question is accepted, Lilly's unreversed day/night Part of Fortune formula, a substantive version of the destructive/impeding planet or abscissor, his practical definition of a third-house short journey, historical definitions of decumbiture and crisis, a lunar-motion refinement of critical days, and a direct definition of planetary or unequal hours.

A significant internal inconsistency is preserved rather than normalized away: Book I gives seventeen degrees for under the Sun's beams and seventeen minutes for cazimi, while the Seventh-House aphorisms in Book II give twelve degrees and sixteen minutes respectively.

Book III adds Lilly's rectification vocabulary, natal longevity terminology with his explicit cautions, Lord of the Geniture, Magistry, primary Direction, annual Profection, Solar Revolution, Transit, Alfridary and several minor aspects. `Radix` is retained as the root/original figure rather than flattened into a synonym for the abstract concept of nativity.

The worked nativity is especially useful methodologically: Lilly repeatedly compares directions with profections, revolutions and transits, allowing concurrence or contradiction among techniques to strengthen, weaken or defer a promised event. Its long yearly forecast catalogue is therefore kept chiefly as evidence of method rather than as hundreds of independent definitions.
