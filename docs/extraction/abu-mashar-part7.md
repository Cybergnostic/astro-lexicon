# Abū Maʿshar — Part VII sequential extraction

Source: *The Great Introduction to Astrology*, ed. and trans. Keiji Yamamoto and Charles Burnett, Brill, 2019.

Status: **Part VII, Chapters 1–9 reviewed completely in reading order.**

## Chapter 1 — planetary conditions in themselves

The chapter expands the condition vocabulary substantially: motion relative to apogee, apparent light and size, numerical/calculational increase and decrease, actual speed relative to mean motion, latitude, and `domain` / ḥayyiz. The domain passage is especially important because it is a compound rule involving planetary gender, sign gender, day/night, and position above or below the earth, with Mars treated as an explicit exception. This should not be silently flattened into a generic later sect definition.

## Chapter 2 — conditions relative to the Sun

Abū Maʿshar gives elaborate phase sequences: seventeen conditions for the superior planets, sixteen for Venus and Mercury, and sixteen for the Moon. `In the heart`, burning, under the rays, easterliness/westerliness, stations, direct motion and retrogradation are separated carefully. Solar thresholds are planet-specific rather than one universal distance. Venus receives a special visibility exception because extreme latitude may keep it visible even near the Sun.

`Dastūrīya` repeatedly appears in connection with strong easterliness and the ability to give the planet's greatest years, but the chapter does not supply a clean independent definition. It is therefore retained as a cautious source-specific entry rather than normalized from later translations.

## Chapters 3–4 — bodily power, conjunction and mixture

Chapter 3 assigns the power of a planet's body before and behind it: Sun 15°, Moon 12°, Saturn/Jupiter 9°, Mars 8°, Venus/Mercury 7°. These ranges are used in Chapter 4 when judging the strength of bodily conjunction and the mixing of planetary qualities.

Chapter 4 makes an important conceptual distinction between bodily conjunction and qualitative mixture. Planets preserve their own essences; what becomes mixed are their qualities. The stronger planet is more manifest in the resulting action. The chapter also preserves competing arguments over whether conjunction of the two malefics can moderate them or intensify their maleficence; both views should remain attributable rather than harmonized.

## Chapter 5 — aspect, application and the twenty-one conditions

This is one of the most lexicon-dense chapters in the whole book. Abū Maʿshar explicitly announces twenty-one conditions following aspect/application/separation. The reviewed vocabulary includes:

- aspect
- application
- separation
- solitary motion
- wildness
- transfer
- collection
- reflecting the light
- prohibition
- giving nature
- giving power
- giving two natures
- giving management
- returning
- refrenation
- resistance
- evasion
- cutting the light
- favour and recompense
- reception
- natural application

Application is developed by conjunction, aspect in longitude, latitude, and a non-aspecting `natural application`. Solitary motion and wildness are distinct: solitary motion means no new application after separation while remaining in the sign; wildness means no planet aspects the planet at all.

Natural application is especially important historically. Abū Maʿshar says it was used in some nativities and questions and attributes it to old Persian, Babylonian and Egyptian books. It is built from the same degree-pair structures previously called natural opposition and natural sextile.

Reception is not reduced to mutual exchange of houses. The chapter treats house and exaltation as strongest, minor dignities as weaker unless combined, allows reception by aspect without application, and then distinguishes strong, middling and weaker forms.

## Chapter 6 — fortune, weakness and corruption

The chapter assembles a large condition matrix for planetary fortune, strength, weakness and misfortune. It includes dignity, joy, domain, speed, light, reception, solar condition, cardinality, retrogradation, fall/detriment, dark degrees, the burnt path, besiegement and other conditions.

`Besieging` is explicitly twofold: enclosure by malefics/rays before and behind, or malefics/rays occupying the second and twelfth relative signs. Benefic or solar intervention can release the affliction; benefic besiegement is treated as especially fortunate.

The `corruption of the Moon` is an elevenfold source-specific list. It should be represented as a historical lunar-condition doctrine, not as one generic modern 'afflicted Moon' category.

## Chapter 7 — casting rays

A mathematical technique projects sextile, quartile, trine and opposition rays through right and oblique ascensions while accounting for a planet's distance from the cardines. Abū Maʿshar attributes the procedure to Ptolemy, but the modern editors explicitly note that this attribution is historically incorrect. The database therefore stores the technique and the attribution separately from the editorial correction.

## Chapter 8 — fardārs and planetary years

The fardār periods are given as Sun 10, Venus 8, Mercury 13, Moon 9, Saturn 11, Jupiter 12, Mars 7, Head 3 and Tail 2 years, totalling 75 years.

A separate doctrine assigns each planet four classes of years: greatest, great, middle and small. These are not being merged with fardār periods.

## Chapter 9 — proper planetary indications

The chapter is a very large catalogue of Saturnine, Jovial, Martial, Solar, Venusian, Mercurial and Lunar indications: qualities, occupations, social groups, behaviours, activities, places, crafts and other correspondences.

These lists are retained as source evidence and author-specific interpretation material. Every individual occupation or ordinary noun is **not** being promoted to a standalone lexicon entry. The chapter itself warns that all listed indications of one planet are not expected to appear together in one person; actual manifestation depends on the planet's condition and place.

## Editorial observations

1. `Domain` / ḥayyiz in this Part must remain source-specific until compared carefully with hayz, halb and related later terminology.
2. `Dastūrīya` is genuine technical vocabulary but under-defined here; preserve the uncertainty.
3. The planetary bodily-power ranges are a historical orb doctrine, but Abū Maʿshar's own framing should be retained.
4. Solitary motion, wildness and void-of-course are not automatically synonyms.
5. Natural application is explicitly non-aspecting and must remain distinct from ordinary application.
6. `Cutting the light`, `prohibition`, `resistance`, and `evasion` are close but separately defined conditions; do not collapse them.
7. Reception is broader and more graded here than a simple mutual-dignity formula.
8. Chapter 4 preserves genuine doctrinal disagreement about the malefics; disagreement is data.
9. Casting of rays and its attribution to Ptolemy need separate source/editorial layers.
10. Planetary indication catalogues are evidence, not an excuse to generate hundreds of low-value lexicon entries.
